Civil engineering / Study guide

Foundations

The maths and physics you need before civil engineering: algebra, geometry, trigonometry, basic physics and units.

What you will learn

In this foundational stage, you'll build the mathematical and physical knowledge required for civil engineering: algebra, geometry and physics form the backbone of all engineering disciplines.

By the end of this stage, you'll be able to:

  • Solve algebraic equations and systems of equations
  • Understand geometric concepts and spatial reasoning
  • Apply Newton's laws and basic physics principles
  • Perform accurate measurements and unit conversions
  • Analyze forces and motion in engineering contexts

How to study

  1. 1. Learn the theory

    Watch video lectures and read textbooks on each topic. Take notes on key concepts and formulas.

  2. 2. Practise problems

    Solve 20–30 practice problems for each topic. Start with easier problems and progress to harder ones.

  3. 3. Apply it to engineering

    Connect each concept to real civil engineering applications such as bridges and buildings.

  4. 4. Ask AI

    Use the LEARNcraft AI chat to clarify confusing topics, get problem hints, or discuss real-world examples.

Weekly study plan

A suggested schedule: 5–8 hours a week for 4 weeks.

Week 1: Algebra review & equations

Master linear and quadratic equations. Topics: solving for unknowns, systems of equations, graphing linear functions. Practice: 25 problems.

Week 2: Geometry, trigonometry & units

Learn properties of shapes, triangles, and trigonometric functions. Understand unit systems and conversions. Practice: 30 problems.

Week 3: Physics fundamentals

Newton's laws, forces, motion, energy, and work. Apply concepts to real structures and systems. Practice: 30 problems.

Week 4: Engineering applications & review

Civil engineering problems combining all previous topics. Complete a practice exam and review weak areas.
