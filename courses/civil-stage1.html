Math for Engineers

Master the mathematical fundamentals required for advanced civil engineering study.

Linear Equations & Algebra
  • Solving linear equations (one and two variables)
  • Systems of linear equations
  • Quadratic equations and factoring
  • Exponential and logarithmic functions

Why it matters: Nearly every structural calculation involves algebraic manipulation. You'll need this constantly.

Functions & Graphs
  • Understanding function notation
  • Plotting and interpreting graphs
  • Polynomial, rational, and piecewise functions
  • Transformations and compositions

Application: Load diagrams, stress-strain relationships, and performance graphs all use function concepts.

Trigonometry
  • Right triangle trigonometry (sin, cos, tan)
  • Unit circle and radians
  • Trigonometric identities and equations
  • Applications in vectors and forces

Critical for Civil Engineering: Analyzing forces, slopes, angles, and structural geometry all rely on trigonometry.

Units & Conversions
  • SI (metric) and imperial units
  • Dimension analysis
  • Converting between unit systems
  • Significant figures and precision

Essential: Mistakes in unit conversion have cost companies millions and caused structural failures. Master this.

Physics Fundamentals

Understand force, motion, and energy principles that govern structural behavior.

Force & Motion (Newton's Laws)
  • First Law: Objects in motion stay in motion (inertia)
  • Second Law: F = ma (force, mass, acceleration)
  • Third Law: Action-reaction pairs
  • Applications to static equilibrium

Why it matters: Structures must remain in equilibrium. This is the foundation of structural analysis.

Mass vs Weight
  • Mass: Amount of material (constant everywhere)
  • Weight: Force due to gravity (W = mg)
  • Calculating loads on structures
  • Dead loads vs live loads

Critical distinction: Civil engineers design for specific weights and loads. Getting this wrong leads to failure.

Vectors & Vector Addition
  • Magnitude and direction
  • Component form (x, y, z)
  • Vector addition and subtraction
  • Dot and cross products

Application: Forces on structures are vectors. You'll combine and analyze them constantly.

Gravity & Acceleration
  • Gravitational acceleration (g = 9.81 m/s²)
  • Free fall and projectile motion
  • Centripetal acceleration
  • Calculating impact forces

Engineering Basics

Introduction to civil engineering concepts and structural thinking.

What is Civil Engineering?
  • History and major disciplines
  • Structural, geotechnical, transportation, water resources
  • Environmental and construction engineering
  • Real-world impact and scale

Perspective: Civil engineers build the infrastructure society depends on. Your work lasts decades or centuries.

Types of Structures
  • Buildings (skyscrapers, houses, commercial)
  • Bridges (beam, cable-stayed, suspension, arch)
  • Roads and transportation infrastructure
  • Dams, canals, and water systems
  • Underground structures (tunnels, basements)
Loads (Dead vs Live)
  • Dead Load: Permanent weight (structure + fixed elements)
  • Live Load: Temporary weight (people, furniture, snow, wind)
  • Environmental loads (earthquakes, temperature)
  • Load combinations for design

Key: Structures must handle worst-case load combinations safely.

Safety Factors
  • Why safety factors exist
  • Factor of Safety (FoS) = Capacity / Load
  • Typical safety factors in civil engineering
  • Risk management and liability

Remember: A safety factor of 1.5 means the structure can handle 1.5x the expected load before failing.

Problem Solving & Tools

Develop engineering problem-solving skills and learn essential tools.

Rearranging Formulas
  • Isolating variables
  • Working with complex equations
  • Dimensional consistency checking
  • Common formula mistakes to avoid

Practice: You'll manipulate formulas hundreds of times. Get comfortable now.

Solving Word Problems
  • Understanding the problem
  • Identifying given and unknown information
  • Drawing diagrams and visualizing
  • Setting up equations and solving systematically
Using Calculators
  • Scientific calculator basics
  • Order of operations (PEMDAS)
  • Rounding and significant figures
  • Common calculator errors
Graph Reading & Interpretation
  • Axes, scales, and units
  • Extracting data from graphs
  • Identifying trends and relationships
  • Understanding slope and area under curves

Civil Engineering: Graphs show material properties, performance data, and design constraints. Learn to read them fluently.
